Identity · mark selection

Fifteen marks went in. Six are worth your time.

Five designers each took a different idea about what ClipCatalyst actually does, drew until something survived, then rendered every mark at true favicon size and looked at it. Three judges scored the results independently — one on whether it holds together at 16 pixels, one on whether it means anything to a stranger, one on craft and whether it could be confused with OpusClip or VEED. Nobody saw the others' scores.

Every mark below is shown the way a logo is really used: tiny, in one flat colour, on both a dark and a light ground. That is where marks die.

Why it wins

The outer corners are rounded and the cut edges are square, so your eye can put the three pieces back together into one rounded rectangle — the source video. It is built on a 1.5-unit module, which means every edge lands exactly on a pixel boundary at favicon size: measured, 4% of its pixels go grey at 16px, against 22–55% for everything else here. Because the three pieces sit on no shared baseline, it refuses to read as a bar chart — the trap that killed three rival marks.

The honest case against

The story it is sold on — one slab, cut apart — is invisible unless someone tells you. Cold, a stranger reads "three tall shapes, moving", which is still true and still on-message, but it is a weaker claim than the idea on paper. It also leans on "Clip" and says nothing about "Catalyst".

Why it nearly wins

It is the only mark whose meaning survived a cold read. Shown with the name hidden, the judge wrote down "wide block, tall block" — and the geometry is literally exact: 12.8×7.2 and 7.2×12.8, a true 16:9 and a true 9:16 of identical area. That is your product stated as geometry, and no competitor in this category says anything about aspect ratio. It is the most defensible territory on offer.

The honest case against

Two rounded rectangles is a very small amount of drawing. It never says video, it has no energy, and at a glance it can read as a layout or grid icon from a system icon set. It states the mechanism beautifully and the personality not at all.

The other four worth seeing

Each was the strongest survivor of its territory. Large in brand violet, then at true 16 pixels beside it — because that second one is what people will actually look at every day.

Splice Arch

Splice's symmetric sibling — the best-scoring moment lifted clear of the other two. Centres perfectly in an app icon, and scored 8.0 at 16px. Slightly closer to the bar-chart look Splice escapes.

Peak

Every moment scored, one of them won — so it grew into a 9:16 clip while the losers stayed as stubs. The centre is a true 9:16 rectangle. Read cold as "a phone flanked by two pills", which is closer to the truth than most managed.

Reframe

Turn's better-argued cousin: the 16:9 source drawn hollow, the 9:16 output drawn solid — the frame emptied, the clip filled. A genuinely good second thought, at the cost of a thin stroke that softens as it shrinks.

Hold

The one monogram that earned itself: the C is a viewport, and its counter holds an upright 9:16 clip. Technically the most robust mark in the whole set — the only one with no 1-pixel gaps at 16px. But a C in a circle is the most crowded shape in software.

How to actually decide

01

Look at this page on your phone and squint. The mark you can still identify while squinting is the one that will work on a browser tab, an app icon, and a TikTok profile picture. That is the test that matters most and it takes five seconds.

02

Ask which story you want to own. Splice says one video becomes many clips. Turn says landscape becomes vertical. Peak says we find the best moment. All three are true about your product; they are not equally easy to draw, and they lead to different marketing.

03

Do not pick for the pitch deck. The mark's real job is being recognised at 16 pixels next to nine other tabs. Anything that only looks good at 256 pixels is a poster, not a logo.

Tell me which one and I will build the full identity around it: the mark in the navbar and footer, a real favicon set, the app icon, an open-graph card for link previews, and the wordmark lockup — replacing the lightning bolt everywhere it currently appears. If none of these land, say what is wrong with the closest one and I will take that territory further rather than start over.
